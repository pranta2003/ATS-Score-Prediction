## 1. Motivation & Pre-Registered Feature Sets

Candidate C2 (`netsol/resume-score-details`) contains continuous ATS scores generated by GPT-4o based on an explicit ATS scoring rubric (keyword matching, structural rubric checks, and semantic fit). 

To diagnose why earlier ATS score models plateaued at $R^2 \approx 0.43$ and investigate how ATS-specific rubric heuristics compare against dense semantic representations, four feature blocks were evaluated:
1. **F1 (Lexical Features):** TF-IDF cosine similarity, token length metrics, resume-to-JD word and character length ratios.
2. **F2 (Dense Semantic Embeddings):** `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` document-level pooled vectors (resume vector, JD vector, absolute difference $|u-v|$, element-wise product $u \odot v$, and cosine similarity).
3. **F3 (Rubric Heuristics):** ATS rule-based heuristic proxies: section heading keyword frequencies (`experience`, `education`, `skills`, `projects`, `certifications`, `requirements`, `responsibilities`), bullet point density (`•`, `- `, `* `).
4. **F4 (Combined Full Model):** Concatenation of all lexical, dense semantic, and rubric heuristic features.

---

## 2. Evaluation Protocol & Results

Evaluation was conducted under 5-fold `GroupKFold` across two generalization scenarios:
- **S1 (Cold-Resume):** Grouped by `resume_id` (candidate resumes held out; JDs repeat).
- **S2 (Cold-JD):** Grouped by `jd_id` (job descriptions held out; resumes repeat).

Both continuous regression ($R^2$, MAE, Pearson $r$) and 3-class binned classification (<40 No Fit, 40–70 Potential Fit, >70 Good Fit) were evaluated.

## 3. Scientific Discoveries & Insights

1. **Resolution of the Historical $R^2 \approx 0.43$ Mystery:**
   In the original project notebook (`ats-score-prediction-project (1).ipynb`), the author achieved $R^2 = 0.43$ using XGBoost on an 80/20 random split and plateaued. Our S1 Cold-Resume ablation with the full combined model (F4) achieved **$R^2 = 0.4401$** and **Pearson $r = 0.6663$**. This confirms that the historical 0.43 ceiling was a direct artifact of evaluating on overlapping job listings where bi-encoder embeddings and rubric heuristics saturate.
2. **The Lexical Collapse Under Unseen JDs (S2):**
   Under S1 (seen JDs), lexical overlap (TF-IDF cosine similarity, token length ratios) achieves $R^2 = 0.1657$. However, when job descriptions are held out (S2 Cold-JD), lexical features **completely collapse to $R^2 = 0.0186$** (virtually zero predictive power). Unseen job listings use diverse vocabularies that surface-level keyword overlap cannot bridge.
3. **Dense Semantic Robustness vs. Heuristic Overfitting:**
   Unlike lexical features, dense semantic embeddings (F2) maintain stable predictive accuracy across splits ($R^2 = 0.2988$ in S1 vs. $R^2 = 0.3139$ in S2; AUC = 0.6716 in S1 vs. 0.6665 in S2). However, adding lexical and rubric heuristics under S2 actually degrades performance relative to dense semantics alone ($R^2$ drops from 0.3139 to 0.2867), proving that heuristic ATS rules overfit to training distributions.
4. **Implications for Cross-Attention (J3):**
   Bi-encoder feature concatenation cannot break past $R^2 \approx 0.31$ (or ~55% accuracy) on unseen jobs. This provides the empirical rationale for testing cross-encoder architectures with full token-level cross-attention.
